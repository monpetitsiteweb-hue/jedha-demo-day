Trained on the past, judged only on later months it never saw: a temporal split, not random.
TRAINING, the model learns here (the past) · 80%
TEST, judged here (unseen) · 20%
How we decide win or loss: we pretend to buy, then watch the next hour. If the price hits our target (+0.95%) first it's a win; if it hits our safety limit (−0.5%) first, or nothing happens, it's a loss.

The models

From the simplest to the most advanced

Why not accuracy? Only ~10% of moments win, so always saying “loss” scores 90%, useless. We judge on F1 and AUC. Random Forest is the best single model (0.870), but the XGBoost + LSTM ensemble does better (0.897).
Rows = what really happened · columns = the model's call. At 0.5 the LSTM over-flags wins (high recall, low precision), hence the high 0.97 bar in production.

Fewer features, same power

We cut 78 features down to 10

We ranked every feature by importance and retrained with fewer and fewer. Ten features match the full seventy-eight, so we keep only those.
Why this matters. The live service only receives 5-minute candles. The features we dropped needed outside data (1h/4h history, a fear-and-greed index) the service can't reproduce exactly in real time.

The 10 we kept are 100% pure-5m, so training and production see exactly the same inputs. No train/serving mismatch.

THE AI MODEL
FastAPI service on Railway
The engine executes the buy only if the probability clears its confidence bar. The service turns the candles into indicators and runs an XGBoost + LSTM ensemble, answering in milliseconds. And if the AI service is ever unreachable, the engine falls back to its own rules, so a model outage never stops trading.
Note: the model comparison in the earlier slides is my offline analysis. Production currently runs an XGBoost + LSTM ensemble; deploying the analysis findings is the next step.
